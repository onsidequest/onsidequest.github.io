# Motivation

- Scaffolding programs enhance LM performance, but they are still designed by hand
- For a fixed Lm and task distribution, designing a scaffold is itself and optimzation problem, so an LM could optimize its own scaffold and repeat the process
- Prior work optimized prompts or fine tunes weights, but none lets a fixed LM recursively improve the scaffold that calls it

## Finding

- **Self-Improvement works with GPT-4**
- On the parity task, test meta-utility rises from about 61% to over 70% within three iterations, beating a greedy iterative baseline at 64.2%

- **The improved improver transfers to unseen tasks**
- Optimized only on the parity task, it beats the ssed improver on all five new tasks with no further self-improvement

- **It only works with a strong model**
- With GPT-3.5 and Mixtral, performance declines over iterations, and only 12% of GPT-3.5 runs gained at least 3%

- **GPT-4 proposed know search strategies, some published after its training cutoff**
- Beam search, genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, bandit-based selection, temperature scheduling, part-by-part improvement

- **The model bypasses constraints on its own**
- Improvers that ignore budget score higher, so the selection step favors them
- Budget constraints are removed for simplication
- The sandbox is disabled in 0.42% of GPT-4 generations versus 0.12% for GPT-3.5

## Method

![STOP self-improvement pipeline]({{ '/assets/img/stop/stop-pipeline.png' | relative_url }})
*Figure 3 of the paper. Each improver improves its own code, scored by the meta-utility ($$\tilde{u}$$ in the figure, $$\hat{u}$$ in the text).*

- **Only the scaffold changes**
- The improver $$I$$ is a Python program: given a solution $$s$$ and a utility $$u$$, it calls the LM $$L$$ and returns $$s' = I(u, s, L)$$, ideally with $$u(s') \gg u(s)$$
- The LM (GPT-4) stays a frozen black box, so this is not full RSI

- **Seed improver $$I_0$$**
- Asks the LM for 6 candidate improvements and returns the one with the highest utility
- Kept simple on purpose, because the improver's own code gets nested inside its prompt

- **Meta-utility $$\hat{u}$$ scores an improver**
- Average downstream utility after improving each training task $$(u, s) \in D$$ with $$I$$:

$$
\hat{u}(I) = \frac{1}{\lvert D \rvert} \sum_{(u,\, s) \in D} u\big(I(u, s, L)\big)
$$

- The improver sees only a grey-box description of $$\hat{u}$$ (an average over tasks), not the tasks themselves

- **Recursion: the improver improves itself**
- Pass the improver its own code as the solution and $$\hat{u}$$ as the utility, for $$T$$ rounds:

$$
I_t = I_{t-1}\big(\hat{u},\, I_{t-1},\, L\big), \qquad t = 1, \dots, T
$$

- $$\hat{u}$$ rewards improving downstream solutions, not improving improvers; the paper assumes the two go together
